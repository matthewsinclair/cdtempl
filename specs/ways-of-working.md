# Ways of working — specification

## What it is

**How the venture operates: who decides, how work moves, and what the rhythms are.**
Not a values statement and not an aspiration — a description a new joiner could follow
in their first week without asking anyone which meeting matters.

The failure mode is specific and universal: this document gets written once, describes
an intention, and is never true. **It must describe what happens**, with the intended
changes marked as changes.

## The standard shape

| Part | Carries |
|---|---|
| Decision rights | Who decides what, by role, including what needs nobody's approval |
| Cadence | The meetings and cycles that exist, each with its purpose and its output |
| Work tracking | Where work lives, in what states, and who moves it |
| Communication | Which channel for which kind of thing, and expected response times |
| Escalation | What happens when something is blocked or disputed |
| Onboarding | The first-week path, which is the test of whether the rest is true |

## Describe what happens, mark what is intended

**A document mixing current practice with intended practice is worse than either
alone**, because a reader cannot tell which parts to follow. Where the venture intends
to change something, say so explicitly: *today X, from March Y*. That is information;
an undated aspiration written in the present tense is a document nobody trusts twice.

**Decision rights are the part that is actually load-bearing.** Cadence and tooling
change often and cheaply. Who can decide a thing without asking is the question that
stalls work when it is unanswered, and it is the section most often reduced to a
diagram of reporting lines, which answers a different question.

## Definition of done

- Decision rights name what needs **no** approval, not only what needs approval
- Every recurring meeting states its output — a meeting without one is a finding
- Escalation names a person or role, not "the team"
- Anything aspirational is dated and marked as not-yet-true
- The onboarding path is written and has been walked by someone new since

## What the brief must carry

An honest account of current practice, including the parts nobody is proud of. A ways
of working document assembled from best practice describes a venture that does not
exist, and its first reader — a new joiner — discovers the gap immediately.

Where decision rights are genuinely unsettled, record that as unsettled. Naming an
owner who has not agreed to be one is worse than the gap.
