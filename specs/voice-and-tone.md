# Voice & tone guide — specification

## What it is

**The rules that make two people writing on different days sound like one venture.**
Voice is what does not change; tone is what does. A guide that only gives adjectives
gives nothing — "friendly, professional, human" describes almost every brand ever
written down and constrains no sentence.

The deliverable is **decisions plus evidence**: the choices made, and enough worked
examples that someone can apply them to a sentence the guide never anticipated.

## The standard shape

| Part | Carries |
|---|---|
| Voice | The three or four traits that hold everywhere, each with what it *rules out* |
| Tone by situation | How the voice shifts — an error, a confirmation, a sales page, an apology |
| Do and don't | Paired rewrites, the same sentence wrong then right |
| Vocabulary | Words used, words never used, and the ones people get wrong |
| Mechanics | Sentence case or title case, serial commas, numerals, contractions, dates |

## Adjectives are not the deliverable

**Every trait must carry what it forbids**, or it is decoration. "Direct" is useless;
"direct — we do not soften a refusal into a maybe" can be applied. The test for a
finished guide is whether two writers, given the same brief and the guide, produce
prose a reader would take for one author.

**Paired rewrites are the highest-value part** and the part most often skipped. A
rule stated abstractly is interpreted; a rule shown as *this sentence became that
sentence* is copied.

## Definition of done

- Every voice trait names what it rules out, not only what it favours
- At least four tone situations, including **at least one where something has gone
  wrong** — that is where voice is tested and where guides are usually silent
- Paired do/don't rewrites, using real sentences from the venture's own material
- A vocabulary list with the never-use words and the reason for each
- Mechanics settled — sentence case, contractions, numerals, dates — so nobody
  relitigates them per document

## What the brief must carry

Two samples: something already written that everyone agrees sounds right, and
something that sounds wrong. Adjectives supplied without samples produce a guide that
describes any brand at all. Also the never-use list, which is usually where a venture
has the strongest and least documented opinions.

## Notes

Reciprocal with `positioning-icp-personas` rather than dependent: who you are talking
to shapes how you sound, and how you sound is part of what you are claiming to be.
Neither can be finished first.
