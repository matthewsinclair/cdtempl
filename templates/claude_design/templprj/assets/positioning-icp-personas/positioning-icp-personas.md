## 1. Positioning statement

For **[target customer]**
who **[problem or need, as they would describe it]**,
**[venture name]** is a **[market category]**
that **[key benefit, in their terms]**.
Unlike **[primary alternative]**,
we **[primary differentiator]**.

> *Target customer* is a role in a kind of organisation, not a market segment.
> "Heads of finance at 50–500 person SaaS companies", not "mid-market".
>
> *Problem* is stated as it is experienced. If it can only be described as the
> absence of your product, it is not yet a problem.
>
> *Category* is the shelf you are asking to be put on. Claiming an existing one
> is almost always right at this stage; it makes the differentiator carry the
> weight, which is where it belongs. If you are claiming a new one, say why below.
>
> *Primary alternative* is most often a spreadsheet, an intern, or nothing at
> all. Name what they actually do today.
>
> *Differentiator* must be verifiable by a customer within a trial.

### The category we are claiming

**[category name]**

- Existing category, or new: **[existing / new]**
- Why this one: [reason]
- What we are giving up by claiming it: [the comparison you now invite]

### What we are not

- [something you are plausibly mistaken for, and the distinction]
- [something you have decided not to be, and why]
- [an adjacent category you will be asked about]

> A positioning with no exclusions has not been decided. This section is the
> test of whether section 1 is real.
