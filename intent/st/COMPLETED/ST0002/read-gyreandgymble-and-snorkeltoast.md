# Reading the first two exports -- Gyre and Gymble, and snorkeltoast

Read 30 July, after the four exports landed and were unzipped by hand into `design/system/`. These two were read first because neither is touched by the ADR / ST / WP renumbering that Lamplight and Baize are going through.

**Both documents are accurate.** Every structural claim was checked against the tree rather than taken on trust, and every apparent discrepancy reconciled to zero. That matters more than it sounds: these are the documents the conversion briefs get written from, and an inventory that overstates its tree would send the next round after material that is not there.

Both deltas that looked like errors were the inventory being more precise than a naive count, not less.

## What Gyre and Gymble delivered

**It did not follow its brief, and the result is more useful than compliance would have been.** The export brief said at line 14 "Do not restructure anything ... not into the Cdsync asset shape". G&G restructured into exactly that shape: `RETURN.md`, `index.md`, `kit/`, `assets/<slug>/spec.md`, `notes/`. There is no `INVENTORY.md`; the inventory is `## The inventory` inside a 478-line `RETURN.md`, which also carries `## What I left out, and why` and `## Decisions I made that you did not ask me to make`.

Sixteen assets, all form C, 11 complete and 5 partial -- and each partial states why it is partial as a decision rather than as unfinished work. The kit is `tokens.css` plus a `tokens.json` **generated by scanning the drop** for colour literals, so "every literal is declared" holds by construction rather than by counting. 52 hex values, 44 alpha, two declared as deliberate counterexamples.

What it deliberately left out is as informative as what it kept: ~90 photographs (belong in `design/`, outside the drop path), the component library (third-party, regenerable), fonts (licensed), the Organic system it overlays (not its to redistribute), the reading room and its build scripts, and `products.json` at top level -- placed inside `assets/pricing-and-packaging/` specifically so nothing implies it is a design token.

**So G&G needs no conversion.** It needs a `check` run and a ruling on its slugs.

## What snorkeltoast delivered

**A true as-is dump, and it says so in the strongest available terms.** Under `## What I left out of this zip, and why` the answer is one word: **Nothing.** Every file, including intermediates, generated output, `_source/` provenance artwork, `uploads/` screenshots, the brief the export was made against, and the retired `cdsync/` thread with a `RETIRED.md` beside it rather than omitted -- "a retired conversation you can read is worth more than an omission you have to ask about."

The tree is organised **by medium**, not by asset: `brand/`, `venture/`, `print/`, `social/`, `email/`, `prototypes/`, `handoff/`, `docs/`, `lib/`, `assets/`, `_source/`, `uploads/`, `cdsync/`. Nothing in it maps onto a Cdsync asset slug. Its prose estate is one markdown file per **section** (66 across 9 documents), with `docs/bin/build-docs.mjs` failing the build if a guide claims a stale count.

**So snorkeltoast is the real conversion job of the four**, and its brief has to be written as a mapping from medium to asset.

## What these two drops settle

This is the evidence ST0002 said would let the open questions be answered "on the evidence of a real drop". It arrived.

### Decision 2 -- taxonomy. Now measured, not argued

G&G was handed the slug list and reported exactly what it did with it: **3 slugs proposed that are not in the list** (`go-to-market-plan`, `cms-rollout-plan`, `print-collateral`, each with its refused near-miss recorded in `notes/slugs-and-what-they-do-not-cover.md`), **4 slugs stretched rather than split**, and **10 slugs from the list deliberately not created** because their content genuinely sits inside another asset -- "an asset directory holding one copied section of a document is padding".

Against the **built** library at `specs/` (12 files), only 4 of G&G's 16 slugs have a spec: `brand-guidelines`, `design-system-index`, `pitch-deck`, `product-one-pager`. The other 12 would import fine and `check` would report `no entry in the spec library` as advisory.

**The `coverage` field is already carrying the load Decision 2 proposed for it.** Five of the 16 specs use it, and each one is a precise statement of what the asset does and does not contain. That is option two of the three working in practice, in a real drop, without a tool change.

### Finding 5 -- `formats_required`. Absent from a real drop entirely

**`formats_required` does not appear anywhere in G&G's 16 specs, or anywhere in the drop.** The schema CD produced is: `asset`, `name`, `spec_version`, `kit_version`, `form`, `tier`, `group`, `audience`, `status`, `inputs_missing`, `depends_on`, `bundles`, plus `coverage` on five.

And G&G took the question head on without being asked it. No PDF or PPTX exists; `notes/exports-and-staleness.md` argues that **an empty exports directory is more honest than a stale one**, and 11 of 16 assets are marked `complete` regardless. snorkeltoast says the same thing independently: seven PDFs unfiled, every sheet press-ready, `docs/pdf/README.md` explaining why the folder is empty -- filed under "waiting on a click, not a decision".

**Two independent suppliers concluded that an asset can be complete without its formats.** That is a strong answer to finding 5, and it argues `formats_required` is advisory -- or, better, that it does not belong in the header looking binding.

### Finding 6 -- `audience`. Confirmed as two fields wearing one name

G&G's 16 specs use `audience` for **both** meanings at once, in one list:

    audience: ["internal"]
    audience: ["customer", "printer", "developer"]
    audience: ["internal", "jen"]
    audience: ["internal", "investor"]

`customer` / `printer` / `developer` is "which audiences can this serve". `internal` is "who may see this" -- a confidentiality marker. `jen` is a **named person**, a third thing again. `RETURN.md` confirms the confidentiality reading explicitly: nine of sixteen assets are internal, "recorded in each `spec.md`'s `audience` field and set out in `notes/confidentiality.md`".

The field is doing three jobs. Finding 6 understated it.

### Decision 3 -- the leak guard. Two more projects built one; Cdsync still has none

ST0002 finding 2 recorded that Lamplight had built its own leak guard. **Both of these projects have one too.**

- **snorkeltoast has a working implementation**: `docs/bin/build-public-site.sh` copies an allow-list, strips the internal tree from the manifest, then greps the build for internal paths and phrases and **deletes the build if anything leaked**. Its inventory states the reason plainly: *"A rule in a file does not stop a wrong command."*
- **G&G has the rule and knows it is unenforced**: nine of sixteen assets internal, recorded in `audience` and `notes/confidentiality.md`, and then -- *"Nothing in this drop or in the importer enforces it."*

Three of four projects independently built or specified confidentiality enforcement. That is no longer a Lamplight peculiarity; it is a protocol gap, and snorkeltoast has a reference implementation Cdsync could read.

## Two things to carry into the briefs

- **G&G's contact contradiction.** The contact address in the repository *(redacted for publication)* appears **nowhere** in the design programme, which carries a phone number and no email. Every print piece and screen points at the phone. Five-minute fix, but it currently makes repository and design programme contradict each other, and it must be settled before anything is printed.

Exported deliberately and correctly. Anything globbing for `intent/whiteboard` or reading `OUTBOX.md` must not reach it. Whether it stays is explicitly hv's call, not the project's.
